Read this first

The game is animated almost entirely from three bought Mixamo packs. Where a pack clip is longer than the move it was assigned to, the import tooling speeds it up to fit — and 37 of 90 states are being played at 1.8x or more. That, not the rigging, is what makes the game look clumsy.

Every move table in here gives a Target length and a @30fps frame count. An animation keyed to that number plays at x1.00 and the problem disappears.

What is wrong with Salaadin's rig, and what was done

His mesh is mostly cloth: leg geometry stops at the top of the boots and above it there is only coat skirt and cloak. Mixamo fits its skeleton to mesh volume, so where the thigh and pelvis should be it found only fabric. Two marker passes were tried; the second came back symmetric and worse. Four joints are now placed by construction in salaadin_fixrig.py, which moves bones in edit mode — the rest pose changes while the mesh and vertex groups stay put, so Mixamo's weights are kept and nothing is re-weighted.

Bone roll is preserved throughout — a roll angle describes different axes once the head or tail has moved. His arms never showed any of this, because the generated clips key arms with IK and only the legs are copied rest-relative.

No root motion. All movement is code.

applyRootMotion is false on all four fighters. Walking, dash distance, attack lunge and knockback are driven by the character scripts in world units. A clip that travels will be ignored at best and will fight the code at worst.

Animate in place. If a move reads as a lunge — Shield Charge, Veiled Rush — the travel is already in the game's lunge values; the clip supplies the lean and the footwork only.

Four import rules every authored clip must satisfy

RuleWhat breaks without it
Copy the body's avatar
CopyFromOther		 Retargeting through the clip's own auto-built avatar is not an identity even on the same skeleton. Wrist twist does not survive it.
Based Upon: Original Body Orientation infers facing from the posed body. On a bladed guard it read the turned shoulders as forward and yawed the whole skeleton 66.6 deg. The tell is that every bone is off by the same angle — check that before blaming a wrist or a pole vector.
Export an empty beside the armature An armature-only FBX makes the armature the file root, so Blender's 100x lands on the avatar's root. The two avatars then disagree on scale and every stretched joint is silently rewritten — the guard arrived with the elbows 16 deg tighter.
Frame 0 holds the rest pose Blender writes the first exported frame as the file's bind pose, and Unity builds the clip's avatar from it. A clip that opens in the guard makes the guard the T-pose, and every frame returns as a delta from the wrong place.

All four are handled automatically by EXPORT_CLIP inside Salaadin_Studio.blend and by the *_clips.py generators. They are listed because any other export route has to reproduce them.

The round trip

1 · KeyPose the rig in Blender from frame 1.
2 · ExportRun EXPORT_CLIP; the FBX lands in Clips/Custom/.
3 · ImportUnity: Crucible > Animation > Use Mixamo packs.
4 · WirePoint the animator state at the clip; set speed back to x1.00.

The trap that costs a day: Unity caches the skeleton

humanDescription.skeleton stores every bone's position in the .meta, and Unity builds the avatar from that cache, not the file. Re-export an FBX with a joint in a new place and nothing changes at runtime. The tell is that the same hierarchy measures correctly in edit mode and wrong in play mode. Rebuild Player<N> clears the cached arrays before every reimport; anything else that touches a rig must do the same.

How an attack is timed

Every attack is three counts at 60 fps. The hitbox arms the instant startup ends and disarms when the active window closes; one hit per swing. The contact frame is therefore startup + 1 — that is where the blade must be on the opponent, and the tables that follow give it for every move.

STARTUP — wind-up
ACTIVE
RECOVERY — follow-through

Active windows are 3 to 5 frames across the whole roster — 50 to 83 ms. The swing must read as a hit in that window, so the blade should be travelling fastest there, not arriving there.

Target length, and why it is the number that matters

Nothing stretches a clip automatically. Use Mixamo packs computes each state's speed itself — state.speed = clip length / move length — so a clip longer than its move is simply played faster, and the speed is recomputed every time that menu item runs. Editing a state's speed by hand will not survive.

The Target column in the move tables is that divisor: total frames / 60, the length a clip has to be for the computed speed to come out at x1.00. The @30fps column is the same number in Blender frames, which is what to key to.

A move with 25 total frames wants a 0.42 s clip, which is 13 Blender frames. That is short. Fighting-game animation is short; the readability comes from the pose at contact, not from the number of frames around it.

Where to start, and what to watch for

Suggested order

#WorkWhy it is first
1The three reaction clips, per fighter React_Front / Left / Right run up to x3.78. They fire on every single hit, so they are the most-seen animation in the game and the worst-played.
2Dashes, Bjorn and Miyamoto x2.3 to x3.1, and dashes are the defensive core — they fire constantly and read as a twitch.

4Parry_Success and the ripostes x3.33 and x3.47. The payoff beat of the whole defensive game lands as a blur.
5Specials x1.8 to x2.4. Most visible per use, least frequent — real value, but after the above.

Things that will bite

Watch forDetail
Two bone conventions Bjorn and Miyamoto are Tripo rigs with their own names; Richard and Salaadin are mixamorig:*. A clip authored on one will not open on the other. Pack clips cross between them only because they go through Unity's humanoid retargeting.
Porting a pose between rigs Convert, never scale by height. Limb targets belong in fractions of that limb's own reach; height-scaled metres miss. Swing a limb with two-bone IK, not by rotating a bone about its own axis — bone roll differs per rig and the limb comes out sideways.
Elbow poles The pole must sit below the shoulder. Salaadin's was once written level with it and both arms came out winged.
Salaadin's stance LEG_DAMP = 0.55 in the generator eases his legs back toward rest, because the pack's block idle plants the feet 94 deg apart — fine on Richard, the splits on him. Keying legs by hand sidesteps this entirely; do not copy the generated leg values.
Bodies are not in git The four body FBXs and all source meshes are gitignored and live on one machine. They are rebuilt from the Blender scripts. The six weapon FBXs are committed.
The frame data can move Startup / active / recovery live in one table, Editor/CrucibleBalance.cs. Change a move's timing there and its clip is re-fitted the next time Use Mixamo packs runs — so a balance pass silently re-times animation. Re-run that menu item after any frame change, then check the speed.

The file to work in

ArtSource/Blender/Salaadin_Studio.blend opens on his corrected rig in pose mode, falchion and shield mounted, mesh locked, scene at 30 fps. Two text blocks ride inside it: EXPORT_CLIP sends the current action to Unity with all four import rules satisfied, and README repeats the workflow. The export runs on a throwaway copy, so it never touches the animation being worked on.

The equivalent for Richard is the generator richard_clips.py; a studio file for him has not been built yet.
